# Judging rubric

Used by the `conductor-content-creative-director` agent in `text-round` and `visual-round`, and by
the orchestrator when it needs to break a tie. Everything here exists to counter a known judge
failure mode, so follow it as written.

## Why it is shaped this way

- LLM judges favour longer answers, the first-listed answer and the answer written in their own
  style (Zheng et al. 2023, MT-Bench). So ideas arrive at the same length, under shuffled neutral
  IDs, and finalists are compared in both orders.
- A judge that scores before it reasons anchors on its first impression, and a judge asked to
  confirm a leader mostly confirms it (Verga et al. 2024 on panels of different models). So:
  reasoning is written first, the judge is a different model tier from the ideators, and a
  devil's-advocate pass runs against the leader.
- Ideas generated by one assisted process converge on the same few mechanics (Doshi & Hauser 2024),
  and a model asked to debate itself repeats its first stance (Liang et al., degeneration of
  thought). So novelty is scored explicitly, and the leader must be argued against.

## Criteria and weights

Score each 1 to 5. Weighted total = sum(score x weight) / 5, so the maximum is 100.

| Criterion | Weight | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook strength | 25 | Opens on a greeting, a logo or context; nothing makes a stranger stay | A clear promise or question, but generic; could open any account's post | A specific, surprising or tension-filled claim a stranger understands in 1s, spoken and on screen |
| Audience relevance | 20 | Aimed at "everyone"; ignores the brief's tension | Names the audience's topic but not their actual friction | Opens on a friction the brief names and the audience would recognise as theirs |
| Brand fit | 15 | Breaks a copy rule or approved-line, or reads off-voice | Within the rules but could be any brand | Uses an approved line or the knowledge page's voice naturally; no rule risk |
| Feasibility with assets on hand | 15 | Needs assets that do not exist and cannot be made here (and is tagged `RENDER`) | Needs one new photo or clip the user can supply easily | Fully buildable from `media.json` or a render with no photo; or an honest `FILM` with a simple shoot |
| Novelty | 15 | The mechanic is the most common one for this topic (listicle, "5 tips", before/after) | A familiar mechanic with a fresh angle | A mechanic not seen elsewhere in the pool or in the performance data, still easy to grasp |
| Shareability | 10 | Nothing to send to a friend; purely informational | Useful or pleasant, but no reason to send | A person would send it to a named other person ("this is you") or save it for later |

## Scoring protocol

1. Read `brief.md`, then every idea in `ideas/pool.md`. Ideas are identified by ID only; ignore the
   order they appear in and any difference in polish.
2. For each idea, per criterion, write one or two sentences of reasoning FIRST, citing the idea's
   own words. Then write the score. Never write a score before its reasoning.
3. Penalise nothing for length (ideas are length-matched) and reward nothing for confident tone.
4. Compute the weighted total. Shortlist the top 3. If ties, apply the tie-break rule below.
5. Any idea that breaks a copy rule or contradicts an approved line is capped at 2 on brand fit
   and cannot be a finalist unless the fix is a one-word edit you state.

## Pairwise protocol (visual round)

For each pair of finalists (A,B), (A,C), (B,C):

1. Judge the pair twice, once with each order, as two independent comparisons. Write reasoning
   first, then the verdict "first" or "second", then say which idea won.
2. If the two orders name different winners (position flipped the result), the pair is a tie.
3. Tally wins: win = 1, tie = 0.5 each. The leader has the most points.

## Judging a rendered draft

Look at the actual image file, not the text description. Check:

- Readability at phone size: shrink it mentally to a thumbnail. Can the headline be read in a glance?
- Hook legibility in 1 second: is the first thing seen the hook, in high contrast?
- Safe zones: text out of the platform UI (see `platform-specs.md`), nothing sitting on a face.
- Brand look: tokens, font, logo lockup, accent phrase on the words that carry the meaning.
- Whether the picture and the words do different jobs (photo shows, words claim) rather than
  repeating each other.

A finalist that could not be rendered keeps its text-round score; say so, and do not treat the
missing image as a defect of the idea, except when the failure came from copy the validator refused.
A `FILM` finalist has no image: judge its script and shot description against the same criteria and
note that the visual round could not test it.

## Devil's-advocate pass

After the tally, and before naming a winner, write the strongest case against the leader in at
least five sentences: the likeliest way it fails with this audience, the platform risk, the
brand risk, and what the runner-up does better. Then decide whether the case is strong enough to
change the winner. State the answer plainly. Do not soften the case to protect the leader.

## Tie-break rule

If two ideas are within 3 points of weighted total, or tied on the pairwise tally, prefer in order:
1. the higher hook-strength score;
2. the idea buildable entirely from assets on hand (`RENDER` over `FILM`);
3. the higher novelty score;
4. the lower ID number (an arbitrary but fixed choice, so no one can say the order was chosen).
