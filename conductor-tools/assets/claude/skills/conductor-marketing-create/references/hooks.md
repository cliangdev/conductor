# Hooks: three layers, all inside the first second

A hook is what makes a thumb stop. A tagline says what the brand is; a hook shows a moment, an object
or a number the viewer recognises. Write several, score them, keep the best.

## The three layers

A hook is not a line of copy. It has three layers, and all three land inside the first second.

- **Visual**: what frame 1 shows.
- **Text**: what is on screen at 0 s (not faded in later).
- **Sound**: the first second of audio: a spoken line, or a sound that belongs to the action (a
  sizzle, a snap, a page tearing).

Write every hook candidate with all three. "none" is allowed for Sound only on a photo slideshow,
which has no audio of its own. A video with no sound in its first second is missing a layer.

## Frame 1: the "what was that?" test

Frame 1 passes when a stranger would think "what was that?": a concrete subject, already moving,
showing the result or the odd, striking thing.

Frame 1 is never:

- a title card, a logo or lockup, a greeting, or an eyebrow label;
- an empty or static scene;
- raw ingredients, parts or an unfinished state when the finished result exists.

Motion and a concrete subject matter more than a face.

## Rules

- **Result first.** Open on the finished outcome, then show how. Exact numbers beat round ones:
  "11 minutes" over "10 minutes".
- **The product or brand name is never in the hook**: not in the on-screen text, not in the spoken
  line, not in the caption's first line. The product appears in use, as the payoff, never as the
  opener.
- **Curiosity gaps are small and closable.** The payoff lands inside the first third of the piece.
  No clickbait without a payoff.
- **Negative or mistake framing** ("stop doing X") is a strong type, but it needs a real, specific
  mistake the brief or knowledge supports.
- One selling point per line, written the way a person would say it (`voice.md`).

## Write 6 to 10 candidates

Mix the `hookType`s from the library below. Do not write ten versions of one. Each candidate is
`Visual / Text / Sound` plus its `hookType`.

An approved line from the Knowledge page still wins when one fits. Quote it verbatim as the Text
layer; use the candidates to find the Visual and Sound around it, or when none fits.

## Hook library

One row per `hookType`, the tag every Creative carries (`learning-loop.md`). Examples are shapes from
different domains; each must pass `voice.md`'s self-check with your own true detail, and each assumes
its facts are true for that brand.

| hookType | Use when | Visual (frame 1) | Text (0 s) | Sound (first second) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `question` | The viewer can answer in their head, or has been wrong about it | A hand holding two near-identical items, one already circled | "which one is the fake?" | Spoken, same line, mid-thought |
| `bold-claim` | You have a specific, true, surprising statement the record supports | The result already in frame, close up and moving | "this fixed the leak in 4 minutes" | Spoken flat, no build-up |
| `pattern-interrupt` | The scene is odd on purpose, or breaks what the feed looks like | A chair at a long empty table, the camera pushing in | "this chair has been waiting since 9:15" | A single object sound: a scrape, a clock tick |
| `relatable-pain` | The brief names a friction the audience recognises as theirs | The painful moment itself, mid-action (a full inbox scrolling, a tab hunt) | "which tab had the quote?" | Spoken sigh-line, or the sound of the problem (frantic clicking) |
| `how-to` | One mechanism does the work and the result can be shown in seconds | The finished result, then the step that made it | "paste a link. skip the rereading." | Spoken instruction, one sentence, or the action's own sound |
| `number` | A specific figure from the record is the point | The number's proof in frame (a timer stopped, a receipt, a total) | "invoices sent in 4 minutes, not 4 days" | Spoken figure first, or a timer click |
| `curiosity-gap` | A small, closable gap; the answer is in the first third | An object or result partly shown, the missing piece obvious | "the third one is why it works" | Spoken teaser, then a beat before the answer |
| `story` | A real moment with a speaker; dialogue or a confession the brief supports | A person mid-moment, or the thing that happened, already in motion | "'are ghosts supposed to be brown?'" | The quoted line, spoken as said |
| `social-proof` | A real, checkable number of people, reviews or results exists | The real message, review or result in frame, not a logo wall | "4,000 people saved this last week" | Spoken, plain, the figure first |

Wording that carries a number or a result has to be true: check it against the brand's claims rules
and the record before using a line shaped like these.

## Score each 1 to 5

Same scale as the content studio's hook strength: 1 is a greeting or generic claim, 3 a clear promise
that could open any account's post, 5 a specific, surprising line a stranger gets in a second.

- **Hook strength**: would the Text and Sound stop a thumb?
- **Visual**: would frame 1 pass the "what was that?" test? A static, branded, title-card or empty
  frame is 1; a concrete subject already moving, showing the result or the odd thing, is 5.
- **Specificity**: does it name a moment, an object or a number?
- **Competitor test**: could a competitor run this unchanged? If yes, it fails; rewrite or drop it.
- **Reads like a person**: would someone say it out loud to a friend? Run `voice.md`'s self-check;
  a template frame ("X but Y"), a brand word or a line that describes the photo fails.

## Keep 2 or 3

Keep the best two or three, with about 8 words or fewer on the artwork. The rest are spare hooks for
a later piece or the caption. Run the chosen line through the brand's copy rules.

When a hook test is wanted, test hook *types* across different pieces, not the same piece twice
(`learning-loop.md`, "Explore").

## Tagline vs. hook

| Tagline | Hook |
|---|---|
| Smarter scheduling for busy teams | this meeting could've been a link |
| Fresh food, delivered | the oven's still cold. dinner's here. |
| Save money on energy | our own bill dropped $38 last month |
| Learn faster with AI | ordered lunch in Spanish on day 30 |

Check the number and speed rows against the brand's claims rules before using a line like them: a number or a result has to be true.
